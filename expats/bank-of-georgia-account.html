Why Bank of Georgia

Bank of Georgia is one of the two banks that dominate Georgian retail banking (the other is TBC), and its parent company is listed on the London Stock Exchange. For expats the practical advantages are a polished mobile app with full English, wide ATM coverage, and — above all — SOLO, the premium tier that most of our clients choose: your own personal banker, dedicated SOLO lounges instead of regular branch queues, and English service throughout. SOLO carries a monthly fee that is reduced or waived above certain balance levels; your banker will confirm current conditions.

If you are buying property through Tbilisi Home, Bank of Georgia is often the natural pick: purchase payments, currency exchange between USD/EUR and GEL, and later rent collection all run smoothly through one account.

What you can do before you arrive

Bank of Georgia does not offer a public online application for non-residents — but that doesn't mean you have to start from zero at the counter. The slow part of any Georgian account opening is the compliance (KYC) review, and that is exactly the part you can prepare remotely:

  1. Assemble your document pack. Scan everything in the checklist below as clear colour PDFs or photos. This is the single biggest time-saver.
  2. Write down your KYC answers. One or two sentences each: why Georgia, what the account is for, where your money comes from, roughly how much will move through the account monthly. You'll be asked these at the branch — having crisp answers ready makes the meeting short.
  3. Send the pack to Tbilisi Home. We forward it to our private banker at Bank of Georgia, who pre-reviews the file and flags anything compliance is likely to query — for example, if your income documents need one more supporting statement, you'll hear about it while you're still at home.
  4. Tell us your arrival date. We book your appointment with the private banker (SOLO lounge if you're going premium) for a day soon after you land.
  5. On arrival: Georgian SIM, then the bank. You'll need a local number for SMS codes — we help you get a SIM within an hour of landing. Then one branch visit: original passport, signatures, account opened, card ordered.

Document checklist

  • Passport — colour scan of the photo page now; the original at the branch. Use the same passport you'll travel with.
  • Bank statements — the last 6–12 months from your main existing bank account.
  • Proof of income or wealth — whichever applies to you: employment contract or employer letter, pension statement, company ownership/registration documents, tax return, investment portfolio statement, or contract from a property or business sale.
  • Purpose evidence (if buying property) — your preliminary purchase agreement or the listing details of the apartment you're buying. Banks love a concrete, documented purpose.
  • Short CV — occasionally requested from non-residents; one page is fine.
  • Georgian phone number — obtained on arrival (Magti, Silknet or Cellfie; passport is all you need for a SIM).
Compliance notes: answer honestly and specifically — vague purposes like “savings” invite follow-up questions. If you are a politically exposed person, declare it upfront. Cryptocurrency gains as your main source of funds need thorough documentation and can slow or block approval. Bank of Georgia may additionally ask non-residents for a tax residency certificate or evidence of ties to Georgia — another reason the pre-review step is worth it.

The branch visit — what actually happens

With the file pre-reviewed and the appointment booked, the visit itself is straightforward and takes about an hour:

  • The banker verifies your original passport and takes your signature on the application and KYC forms.
  • You confirm your answers on income, purpose and expected turnover — the same ones you prepared at home.
  • Your multi-currency account (GEL/USD/EUR) is opened; for well-prepared files this is often approved the same day, otherwise the compliance review typically takes 2–7 business days.
  • You choose and order your card — standard cards are often issued on the spot or within a few working days; SOLO cards may take slightly longer.
  • The banker sets up the Bank of Georgia mobile app and internet banking on your phone, in English.
  • Expect a small one-time fee for non-resident onboarding and/or card issuance — typically in the range of GEL 10–100 depending on the account tier.

Can I open it without coming to Georgia at all?

Yes. Georgian law allows an account to be opened by a representative holding a notarised (and, for most countries, apostilled) power of attorney, together with a notarised copy of your passport. Tbilisi Home can connect you with a trusted lawyer to act as your representative, and our private banker handles the bank side.

Be aware of the trade-offs: the process takes roughly 2–4 weeks including document couriering, the bank applies stricter compliance checks to POA openings, and some services (like certain card deliveries or limit increases) may still require you to appear in person later. For most clients who are coming to Georgia anyway, the prepare-remotely-plus-one-visit route above is faster and cheaper.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a residence permit or visa to open the account?

No. A valid passport is sufficient — Georgia imposes no residency requirement for personal bank accounts. Citizens of most countries can also stay in Georgia visa-free for a full year, which makes the logistics easy.

Do I need a Georgian address?

Generally no — proof of a local address is not mandatory for opening. If you've rented or bought through us, we can provide the address details banks occasionally like to record.

Is SOLO worth it?

For most of our buyers, yes: you get a named personal banker you can message directly (invaluable when a transfer needs explaining to compliance), lounge service instead of queues, and consistently English-language support. If you only need a simple account for daily spending, the standard tier is perfectly good.

What can go wrong?

The usual culprits are undocumented income, vague answers, or higher-risk citizenship/source-of-funds combinations that trigger enhanced due diligence. Approval is always the bank's decision, not ours — but pre-review catches the large majority of problems before you fly. If one bank declines, another often approves; that's one advantage of our contacts at all three.
