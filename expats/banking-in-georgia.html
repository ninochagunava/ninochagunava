Open a Georgian Bank Account — Start Before You Arrive

A local bank account is one of the first things every foreign buyer, renter and new resident needs in Georgia. If you prepare your documents and application before you land, the whole thing comes down to a single branch visit of about one hour — with a private banker we already work with, at whichever bank you choose.

How it works with Tbilisi Home

Tbilisi Home works with English-speaking private bankers at all three major retail banks in Georgia: Bank of Georgia, TBC Bank and Liberty Bank. In the past we handled the whole account-opening process together with clients after they arrived — which often meant two or three branch visits while the bank's compliance team asked follow-up questions.

There is a better way. Georgian banks will happily open personal accounts for foreigners, but the part that takes time is compliance (KYC) review — the bank checking who you are, where your money comes from and what the account is for. Almost all of that can be prepared remotely, before your flight:

  1. Choose your bank. Read the three guides below, or ask us — we'll recommend one based on your situation. Many clients open accounts at two banks; it costs little and gives you a backup.
  2. Prepare your document pack at home. Passport scan, proof of where your money comes from, and clear answers about what the account is for. Each bank guide has the full checklist.
  3. Send the pack to us. We pass it to the private banker at your chosen bank, who pre-reviews it and tells us in advance if compliance will want anything extra — so surprises happen by email, not at the counter.
  4. We book your appointment. We schedule the branch visit with the private banker for a day shortly after your arrival.
  5. One visit, about one hour. You bring your original passport, sign the forms, get your account (usually GEL, USD and EUR in one) and order your card. Done.
Prefer not to travel at all yet? Georgia also allows fully remote account opening through a notarised power of attorney given to a local representative. It takes longer (typically 2–4 weeks) and banks apply stricter checks, but it is possible — each bank guide explains this option.

What every bank will ask, whichever you choose

Georgian banks follow international anti-money-laundering rules, so all three will want the same core things from a foreign applicant:

  • Your passport — the original at the branch, a clear colour scan beforehand.
  • Source of funds — recent statements (6–12 months) from your existing bank, plus whatever explains the money: employment contract or employer letter, pension statement, business ownership documents, tax return, investment or property-sale records.
  • Purpose of the account — a short, honest, specific answer: “buying an apartment in Tbilisi”, “receiving rent from my property”, “living expenses while residing in Georgia”. If you're buying through us, the preliminary purchase agreement is a perfect supporting document.
  • Expected turnover — a realistic estimate of monthly money in and out.
  • A Georgian SIM card — needed for SMS codes; we help you get one within an hour of arrival.
Good to know: vague answers are the number-one reason applications stall. So is naming cryptocurrency as your main income without documentation. Be specific, be honest, and bring paper — compliance reviews for non-residents typically take 2–7 business days, and a well-prepared file often clears the same day.
