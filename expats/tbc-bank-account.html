Opening a TBC Bank Account: Start Before You Arrive

TBC is Bank of Georgia's great rival and its equal in size, famous for the best banking app in the country — fully in English. It's also the only major Georgian bank that lets foreigners pre-register online, which makes it the easiest of the three to start from abroad. Prepared properly, your account opens in a single one-hour branch visit.

Why TBC Bank

TBC and Bank of Georgia trade places at the top of Georgian banking, and both parent companies are listed on the London Stock Exchange. TBC's calling card is technology: its mobile app is consistently rated the best in Georgia, works fully in English, and handles everything from currency exchange to deposits and bill payments without a branch visit. For expats who live on their phone, TBC usually feels the most familiar.

TBC Concept is the premium tier — a personal banker you can reach directly, stylish Concept spaces instead of regular branches, and priority English-language service. Like SOLO at Bank of Georgia, it carries a monthly fee that is reduced or waived above certain balance levels; your banker will confirm current conditions.

What you can do before you arrive

TBC has a genuine head start over the other banks here: foreigners can begin the application online before ever setting foot in Georgia. Combined with our private-banker pre-review, nearly all of the work happens before your flight:

  1. Pre-register online with TBC. TBC offers online pre-registration for foreign nationals on tbcbank.ge — you submit your basic personal details digitally so the bank already has your file started when you walk in. We'll send you the current link and walk you through it.
  2. Assemble your document pack. Scan everything in the checklist below as clear colour PDFs or photos.
  3. Write down your KYC answers. One or two sentences each: why Georgia, what the account is for, where your money comes from, and a realistic monthly turnover estimate. These are the questions the banker must ask — arriving with crisp answers is what keeps the visit to an hour.
  4. Send the pack to Tbilisi Home. We forward it to our private banker at TBC, who pre-reviews it against compliance requirements and tells us in advance if anything extra is needed.
  5. Tell us your arrival date. We book your appointment with the private banker — at a Concept space if you're going premium — for a day soon after you land.
  6. On arrival: Georgian SIM, then the bank. A local number is required for SMS codes; we help you get a SIM within an hour of landing. Then one visit: original passport, signatures, account opened, card ordered.

Document checklist

  • Passport — colour scan of the photo page now; the original at the branch. Use the same passport you pre-registered with.
  • Bank statements — the last 6–12 months from your main existing bank account.
  • Proof of income or wealth — whichever applies: employment contract or employer letter (remote workers: your contract plus recent payslips or invoices work well), pension statement, company ownership documents, tax return, investment statement, or a property/business sale contract.
  • Purpose evidence (if buying property) — your preliminary purchase agreement or details of the apartment you're buying through us.
  • Short CV — occasionally requested from non-residents; one page is fine.
  • Georgian phone number — obtained on arrival (Magti, Silknet or Cellfie; a passport is all you need for a SIM).
Compliance notes: TBC applies the same international KYC standards as every Georgian bank. Be specific and honest about income and purpose; declare politically-exposed-person status if it applies; and don't present undocumented cryptocurrency gains as your main source of funds. Non-resident files go to a compliance review that typically takes 2–7 business days — a clean, pre-reviewed file is often approved the same day.

The branch visit — what actually happens

  • The banker pulls up your online pre-registration, verifies your original passport and takes your signatures on the application and KYC forms.
  • You confirm your prepared answers on income, purpose and expected turnover.
  • Your multi-currency account (GEL/USD/EUR) is opened, pending any remaining compliance review.
  • You choose and order your card — standard cards are often issued on the spot or within a few working days.
  • The banker sets up the TBC app and internet banking on your phone, in English.
  • Expect a small one-time fee for non-resident onboarding and/or card issuance — typically in the range of GEL 10–100 depending on the account tier.

Can I open it without coming to Georgia at all?

Yes — as at the other banks, a fully remote opening is possible through a notarised (and usually apostilled) power of attorney to a local representative, together with a notarised passport copy. Tbilisi Home can connect you with a trusted lawyer to act as your representative and coordinate with our TBC private banker.

Expect roughly 2–4 weeks end-to-end, stricter compliance scrutiny than an in-person opening, and the possibility that some services will still require a personal visit later. If you're coming to Georgia anyway, pre-registration plus one branch visit is faster and cheaper.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a residence permit or visa?

No — a valid passport is enough, and Georgia has no residency requirement for personal accounts. Citizens of most countries can stay in Georgia visa-free for a full year.

Does the online pre-registration mean my account is approved?

No — it starts your file and saves time at the branch, but the account is only opened after the in-person identity check and compliance review. Think of it as checking in online before a flight: faster boarding, same security.

TBC or Bank of Georgia?

Honestly, both are excellent and most clients would be happy with either. TBC tends to win on app experience and the online pre-registration head start; Bank of Georgia's SOLO tier has the edge in premium lounge service. Many of our clients simply open both — a second account costs little and is a useful backup. We'll happily recommend one for your specific situation.

What can go wrong?

Undocumented income, vague answers, or a higher-risk profile can trigger enhanced due diligence or a decline. Approval is always the bank's own decision — but our pre-review catches most issues while you're still at home, and if one bank declines, another often approves.
